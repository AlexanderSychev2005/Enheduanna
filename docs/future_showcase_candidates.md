## Complaint tablet to Ea-nāṣir

**Why interesting:** the internet's favorite "world's oldest customer
complaint" meme — a real, relatable consumer-complaint letter (bad-quality
copper, rude treatment of a messenger) that reads exactly like a modern
angry email. Strong showcase pull for a general audience.

**Not used in the Kanesh article** — this is Ur, Old Babylonian period
(c. 1750 BC), about the Dilmun copper trade, not Kanesh/Old Assyrian tin
and textile trade. Different region, period, and trade network entirely;
would confuse the Kanesh article's scope. Kept for the diploma's own
showcase section instead (general "damaged text restoration" demo, not
tied to any one region).

**Identifiers (verified 2026-09-06):**
- CDLI: **P414985**
- Museum: British Museum, **BM 131236** (registration W 1953-0411-71)
- Publication: **UET V, 81**
- Currently on display/loan at the Zayed National Museum, Abu Dhabi
  (through ~2027), per Wikipedia — not physically in London right now.

**Corpus status:** present in our raw CDLI-bulk ATF dump
(`data/raw/cdli_bulk/cdliatf_unblocked.atf`, `&P414985 = UET 5, 0081`) but
**not yet in the processed corpus** (no cdli_cat.csv metadata row — same
"catalog gap" pattern as the Cyrus Cylinder). No `#tr.en:` translation line
in the raw ATF, no photo via our own CDLI photo mirror (404, same as
Cyrus) and eBL's own API reports `hasPhoto: false` (only a lineart at
`dl/lineart/P414985_l.jpg`) — but see photo note below, this one might
actually be recoverable unlike Cyrus.

**Translation:** the version below is the one that circulates widely online
(this is what the user pasted in, sourced from the Reddit thread above) —
it matches, phrase for phrase, what web search independently surfaced as
"Oppenheim's translation," so it's very likely genuinely his rather than a
fan rewrite, but **not yet verified word-for-word against the actual 1967
printed book** (Oppenheim, *Letters from Mesopotamia: Official, Business,
and Private Letters on Clay Tablets from Two Millennia*, University of
Chicago Press, 1967) — check a library/scanned copy before citing this as
"Oppenheim 1967" in anything that will be submitted, since what's online
could carry small transcription drift from the print original. Other
translators also exist (Leemans 1960, Van De Mieroop 2010, Diakonoff 1990
in Russian) — worth comparing if the exact wording matters later.

> Tell Ea-nasir: Nanni sends the following message:
>
> When you came, you said to me as follows: "I will give Gimil-Sin (when
> he comes) fine quality copper ingots." You left then but you did not do
> what you promised me. You put ingots which were not good before my
> messenger (Sit-Sin) and said: "If you want to take them, take them; if
> you do not want to take them, go away!"
>
> What do you take me for, that you treat somebody like me with such
> contempt? I have sent as messengers gentlemen like ourselves to collect
> the bag with my money (deposited with you) but you have treated me with
> contempt by sending them back to me empty-handed several times, and that
> through enemy territory. Is there anyone among the merchants who trade
> with Telmun who has treated me in this way? You alone treat my messenger
> with contempt! On account of that one (trifling) mina of silver which I
> owe(?) you, you feel free to speak in such a way, while I have given to
> the palace on your behalf 1,080 pounds of copper, and Šumi-abum has
> likewise given 1,080 pounds of copper, apart from what we both have had
> written on a sealed tablet to be kept in the temple of Shamash.
>
> How have you treated me for that copper? You have withheld my money bag
> from me in enemy territory; it is now up to you to restore (my money) to
> me in full.
>
> Take cognizance that (from now on) I will not accept here any copper
> from you that is not of fine quality. I shall (from now on) select and
> take the ingots individually in my own yard, and I shall exercise
> against you my right of rejection because you have treated me with
> contempt.

**Photo:** unlike Cyrus, Wikipedia explicitly points to **Wikimedia
Commons** having a photo of this exact tablet (there's a Commons category
for it) — freely licensed, unlike CDLI's own gap. Worth checking Commons
directly before assuming we need special permission, the way we'd have to
